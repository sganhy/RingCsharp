Alchemy, with real semantic fit:

Amalgam * — an alloy formed by fusing distinct elements into one; a schema framework that unifies tables, fields, relations, indexes, and constraints into one coherent structure is literally an amalgam. Strong, plain-English-adjacent (not too obscure), short.
Alkahest * — the legendary universal solvent, alchemy's answer to "dissolves anything." A nice metaphor for a driver meant to be a universal, dependency-free conduit to the database. More obscure, harder to say at first glance, but distinctive and unlikely to collide with anything.
Athanor — the alchemist's furnace, used for slow transformation over sustained heat. Fits the "built from scratch over a long-running project" story, if you want the name to reference the effort rather than the artifact.


Geology, with real semantic fit:

Strata * — layered structure, directly mirroring the meta-model you've actually built (Table → Field/Relation/Index/View, Schema above them). Probably the single most literal match to what the code does.
Aquifer — an underground layer that stores and transmits water. Good driver metaphor (stores and moves data), reads as deliberate rather than decorative.
Karst — a landscape carved by dissolution into underground channels and caverns; nice metaphor for connection pooling/routing, more obscure and distinctive, less immediately legible to someone with no geology background.


Geology — same vein as before, further from the well-picked-over words:

Xenolith — a fragment of foreign rock embedded inside another rock. Genuinely literal fit: this project embeds foreign resource data inside the assembly via GetManifestResourceStream.
Breccia — rock made of broken fragments cemented into one mass by a fine matrix. Fits a schema built from many small pieces (tables, fields, relations) bound into a whole.
Gossan — weathered, oxidized rock at the surface that signals an ore deposit below. Fits a metadata layer that points to the real data underneath it.
Saprolite — rock chemically weathered in place, still preserving the original rock's structure. Fits a layer that wraps Postgres while preserving its underlying shape.

Alchemy — rarer than Alembic/Alkahest/Athanor:
5. Cucurbit — the gourd-shaped vessel forming the body of an alembic still. Alchemy-rooted, without Alembic's exact-name collision.
6. Menstruum — a dissolving/extracting solvent, alkahest's lesser-known cousin. Same "universal connector" metaphor.
7. Calx — the ash residue left after calcination. Short and punchy, closer to "Ring"'s length than most of these.
8. Spagyric — an alchemical method of separating a substance into parts and recombining them. Fits "decompose into schema/meta, recombine into a working whole" well.

A different well entirely — structural/architectural terms, since "schema" itself means a structural framework:
9. Trestle — a framework of beams supporting a structure from below. Literal "framework," and outside the two domains you'd already mined.
10. Fascia — a band or layer that binds and supports what's beneath it (architecture and anatomy both use the word). Distinct sound, layered-support metaphor.
